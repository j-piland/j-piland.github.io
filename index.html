Jacob Piland

My research bridges trustworthy AI and empirical AI safety. Much of my work begins with a deceptively simple question: is the model succeeding for the reasons we think it is? I have studied systems that remain accurate while producing misleading explanations, models whose confidence does not reflect their reliability, and specialized tasks where human expertise can help a general-purpose model make better decisions. I enjoy turning those ambiguities into experiments by finding a measurable failure mode, building a way to stress-test it, and developing practical methods that make the resulting system easier to trust.

Before focusing on machine learning, I worked across instructional technology, computational biology, Earth science, and systems engineering. That interdisciplinary path shaped how I approach research: define what a system is actually measuring, test whether its apparent success survives scrutiny, and translate among technical domains without losing sight of the human purpose behind the work.

I am especially interested in empirical AI safety, trustworthy model evaluation, interpretability, human–AI oversight, and research that exposes the gap between what a model appears to be doing and what its behavior actually supports. My CV and publication list provide additional details, and I am always glad to connect with researchers working on related problems.
